\section{Final Thoughts: Does Shor's Algorithm Really Break RSA?}

During this article, you went through all of Shor's algorithm and had a whole
journey with it. I hope that I was able to make you enjoy the journey. You
probably have a lot of questions in your mind now, and one of them would be
that you saw with your own eyes that we have an algorithm that breaks our
usual encryption. So how is our data safe?

Actually, we didn't truly break it. We are assuming that we have a quantum
computer that is able to run Shor's algorithm, and we don't. Shor's algorithm
would need $4100$ logical qubits, or $20{,}000{,}000$ physical qubits to run.

Currently, the main actor in this field is IBM. As of 2023, their most advanced
processor --- the Condor --- reached $1{,}121$ physical qubits, and by 2026
IBM plans to deploy a combined $4{,}158$-qubit system through interconnected
processors. Both figures remain far from the $20{,}000{,}000$ physical qubits
Shor's algorithm would require. But the day we are able to make a quantum
computer with $20{,}000{,}000$ physical qubits, we will definitely be able to
break RSA, so the question is not if we are going to be able to, but
\textbf{when}?
